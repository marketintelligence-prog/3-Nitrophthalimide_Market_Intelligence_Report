Synonyms: 4-nitroisoindoline-1,3-dione; 4-nitro-1H-isoindole-1,3(2H)-dione; phthalimide, 3-nitro-; 3-NPM (trade shorthand) [6][7].

3-Nitrophthalimide is the cyclic imide of 3-nitrophthalic acid. The N-H imide and the nitro group are its two reactive handles. The imide can be acylated or alkylated, and the nitro group can be reduced to an amine. It sits one step below 3-aminophthalimide and two steps below 3-nitrophthalonitrile, so its value lies mainly in what it becomes. Its sister isomer, 4-nitrophthalimide (CAS 89-40-7), is easier to make and cheaper, and the two are easily confused.

Pharmaceutical intermediates

Celgene's patent example makes (S)-pomalidomide through 3-nitrophthalimide [26]. Commercial generic routes described in the literature instead condense 3-nitrophthalic anhydride with 3-aminopiperidine-2,6-dione, then reduce [28]. Apremilast uses 3-acetamidophthalic anhydride or the anhydride-amine route, not this imide [29][30].

Dyes, pigments and electronics

Natco's patent names 3- and 4-aminophthalimide as dyestuff intermediates and fluorescent dyes for fibres [11]. Vikas Industries markets the product for dyestuff and pharma intermediate use [14]. The higher-value route is 3-nitrophthalonitrile, used for phthalocyanines in laser-printer photoconductors, optical media and NIR-absorbing filters [10][25]. Optical-disc demand is mature, so growth depends on NIR filters and security inks.

Chem-Impex also claims polymer, photoresist and analytical uses [12]. These are catalogue statements with no supporting commercial evidence found, so they are excluded from demand estimates.

Luminol and diagnostics

3-Aminophthalimide is described as a stable luminol precursor [24], and ChemicalBook lists it as a luminol raw material [32]. The mainstream and patented routes go directly from 3-nitrophthalic acid via 3-nitrophthalhydrazide [33], so the imide is an alternative, not the default. Sigma tags the imide for diagnostic assay manufacturing, hematology and histology [3].

Research and reference standards

The largest visible use today. Catalogue houses sell it in 5 g to 1 kg packs. TargetMol sells it as a fragment scaffold [34], and HPC Standards lists it as a reference material [16]. All five rows in the screenshot are this segment [TD]. Antifungal activity is mentioned by vendors [4], but no commercial product was found.

Agrochemicals, food, cosmetics and construction: no evidence of use found. Vendor tag "Lurasidone Hydrochloride-10" on ChemicalBook [6] has no route support and is disregarded.
